Mental Health Safety Plan & Vacation Map

PERSONAL SAFETY BLUEPRINT

This section is your gentle guide: what to do, who to call, and how to build a mental health vacation that feels safe, soft, and restorative.

1. Warning Signs

  • Emotional: Feeling hopeless, numb, unusually irritable, or like you want to disappear.
  • Physical: Not sleeping, sleeping too much, headaches, tight chest, racing heart.
  • Behavior: Withdrawing from loved ones, canceling everything, ignoring messages, neglecting hygiene.

2. Coping Tools

  • Grounding: 5–4–3–2–1 senses check, holding something warm, naming colors in the room.
  • Comfort: Favorite blanket, soft music, warm shower, reading a gentle story or scripture.
  • Expression: Journaling, drawing, coloring, writing a letter you don’t have to send.

3. Safe People & Places

  • People: List 2–3 trusted contacts (family, friends, church members) with phone numbers.
  • Places: A quiet room at home, a park bench, a chapel, a library corner.
  • Online: Crisis text lines, supportive communities, uplifting talks or devotionals.

4. When to Call for Help

  • Immediate: If you have a plan to harm yourself or others → call 911 or 988.
  • Escalating: If coping tools aren’t working and thoughts feel louder → text HOME to 741741.
  • Ongoing: Reach out to a therapist, doctor, or spiritual leader for regular support.

Mental Health Vacation Plan

  • 1. Choose Your Theme: “Rest & Restore”, “Creative Retreat”, or “Spiritual Quiet Time”. Let everything match that theme.
  • 2. Set Boundaries: Limit social media, silence stressful chats, say “I’m taking a mental health break this week.”
  • 3. Daily Rhythm: Morning light walk, one nourishing meal, one creative or comforting activity, early bedtime.
  • 4. Safe Structure: Keep emergency numbers visible, tell one trusted person your plan, schedule check-ins.
  • 5. Gentle Activities: Journaling, baking, drawing flowers, reading, watching favorite movies, simple stretching or yoga.
  • 6. Re-entry Plan: On the last day, list 3 small tasks for the next week, not everything at once.

This page is a companion, not a replacement for professional care. If things feel unsafe, please call 911 or 988 right away.

Mental Healing & Wellness Center

Grounding, coping, and emotional support for busy holiday seasons.

Mindfulness for the Holidays

  • Deep breathing: inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 6.
  • 5–4–3–2–1 grounding: notice senses around you.
  • Short daily check‑ins: “What do I need right now?”

Holiday Emotional Check‑In

Coping Skills Library

  • Cognitive reframing: challenge “all‑or‑nothing” thoughts.
  • Sensory calming: soft lights, warm drink, quiet music.
  • Overwhelm plan: step outside, breathe, text a safe person.

Jack Skellington Halloween Safety Plan

Stay spooky—just not risky.

Costume Safety

  • Make sure you can see clearly through masks.
  • Avoid tripping hazards like long trailing fabric.
  • Use breathable materials to avoid overheating.

Trick‑or‑Treating Rules

  • Go with a buddy or trusted adult.
  • Stick to well‑known routes and neighborhoods.
  • Check candy before eating; avoid unwrapped items.

Nighttime Awareness

  • Carry a flashlight or glow sticks.
  • Wear reflective tape on costumes.
  • Stay in well‑lit areas and avoid isolated spots.

Christmas Safety Plan

Cozy, bright, and safe for everyone.

Home Safety

  • Keep trees away from heaters and open flames.
  • Turn off lights before bed or leaving home.
  • Use proper extension cords and avoid overloading outlets.

Travel Safety

  • Check weather and road conditions before driving.
  • Pack blankets, water, and a small emergency kit.
  • Let someone know your route and ETA.

Family Gathering Emotional Safety

  • Set boundaries around topics that feel harmful.
  • Take breaks in a quiet room if overwhelmed.
  • Plan a “safe person” to sit or talk with.

Easter Safety Plan

Soft colors, bright smiles, safe celebrations.

Egg Hunt Safety

  • Mark clear boundaries for the hunt area.
  • Check the ground for hazards before kids run.
  • Ensure adult supervision at all times.

Food Safety

  • Refrigerate eggs promptly after boiling.
  • Be mindful of allergies (nuts, dairy, gluten).
  • Label foods clearly at gatherings.

Emotional Wellness

  • Don’t force participation; let people opt out.
  • Offer quiet activities for those who prefer calm.
  • Check in with yourself: “Is this fun for me?”
